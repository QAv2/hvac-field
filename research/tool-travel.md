# Tool travel for a USAP utility mechanic (McMurdo, summer): findings

Research date: 2026-10-22. Bounded pass, about 20 minutes. Sources are USAP/ASC documents where I could read them. Anything I could not confirm is marked OPEN.

## Checklist (short version)

- [ ] **Weight budget is tight.** The Christchurch-to-McMurdo ice flight allows 85 lb (39 kg) of checked baggage plus a 15 lb (7 kg) carry-on. The ECW gear you wear on the flight is outside that. Plan for about 10 lb of ECW gear to be counted in the budget. Weigh the meter and probe kit before you pack it. Source: ASC-Travel-0010 v12 (July 2023) and Participant Guide Ch. 3.
- [ ] **Tools are not confirmed as "provided."** I found no USAP statement that you must not bring your own tools, and no statement that tools are supplied. Ask Amentum/ASC directly before you pack.
- [ ] **Carry-on the spare batteries, and keep them there.** The TSA rule is that spare lithium cells go in carry-on, not checked. Batteries installed in a meter can likely go in checked bags, but I did not confirm this from TSA. OPEN.
- [ ] **Do Not Freeze (DNF) items do not go in checked or hand-carry bags.** The Participant Guide says DNF equipment goes through the USAP cargo system. Whether a meter or probe set counts as DNF is OPEN. Ask the ASC science cargo office.
- [ ] **Leaving gear on station: there is no written USAP donation policy that I could find.** The informal "skua" pile exists. Anything beyond that needs a written answer from ASC property before you leave it. OPEN.
- [ ] **New Zealand biosecurity.** Clean, dry, and declare any gear that has touched soil or outdoor work, per MPI guidance. I could not fetch the current MPI page, so check it before you arrive. OPEN.
- [ ] **Cold.** Keep batteries warm until use. Expect LCD slowdown in the cold and condensation when you come inside. Details are below, with weak sourcing.

## 1. USAP / ASC baggage rules

**Official: ASC-Travel-0010 "USAP Baggage Allowance," Version 12, July 2023.** Source: https://www.usap.gov/usapgov/travelAndDeployment/documents/5%20ASC-Travel-0010%20baggage.pdf (text extracted locally with pdftotext).

- Commercial leg, Area of Departure to Christchurch (summer): 2 bags up to 50 lb (23 kg) each, plus carry-on.
- Ice flight, Christchurch to McMurdo (summer): 85 lb (38.5 kg) plus carry-on. The sheet says to save room for 10 lb of ECW gear.
- Carry-on, and "must fit into a standard airline sizing box."
- Bags over 62 in (158 cm) total are "oversized" and not reimbursable.
- "Ice flight allowances are strictly enforced; excess baggage must be pre-approved."
- "The USAP reserves the right to delay personal baggage in order to meet safety and cargo requirements."

**Participant Guide, Chapter 3 "How and What to Pack."** Source: https://usap.gov/travelAndDeployment/documents/ParticipantGuide-Chapter3.pdf (text extracted locally)

- "All personnel, summer and winter, have the same luggage weight allowance when flying from New Zealand to McMurdo Station. A maximum of 39 kg/85 lbs for your luggage plus 7 kg/15 lbs for carry-on is allowed for this leg of your journey."
- ECW issue is about 10 kg (22 lb). Items you must wear on the flight do not count. "The rest of the issued clothing (about 3 kg/7 lb) will be considered part of your checked baggage."
- Carry-on limit: 61 x 38 x 23 cm (24 x 15 x 9 in).
- Personal belongings and unworn ECW go as checked baggage on a cargo pallet.
- Sharp objects are not allowed in carry-on. Pocket knives and scissors go in checked bags.
- "Laptops are accepted as hand-carry."
- "Do Not Freeze (DNF) equipment should not be included in your checked or hand-carry baggage. Use the USAP cargo system to protect it from freezing (see Chapter 4)."
- "Other personal equipment. Personal baggage may also include delicate scientific instruments that must be hand-carried."
- Excess baggage: the program does not reimburse it. You can note excess on the Trip Details form, or mail boxes to yourself at the APO address (Ch. 3, Chapter 6 for mail restrictions).
- Hazardous materials "may not be shipped as baggage, mail, or hand-carry" (Ch. 3 and Ch. 4).
- Disposable batteries: "Disposable batteries, such as mercury, alkaline, and lithium cells, are discouraged. Consider buying rechargeable batteries and a charger." (Ch. 3, "What not to pack.")

**Tools.** I found no statement in Chapter 3, Chapter 4, or the 2016-17 employee packet about personal tools. The employee packet search for "tool" returned only a hit about network-penetration software. I did not find "tools are provided, do not bring your own." OPEN.

**Contractor job context (not a tool policy).** Amentum job postings describe the Utility Mechanic role under the Antarctic Support Contract. They say nothing about tools. Examples: https://amentum.dejobs.org/virtual-ata/utility-mechanic/61F3B7C049BA4B7381287BB0A24A3FFC/job and https://www.hays.net.nz/job-detail/utility-mechanics-needed-in-antarctica!-christchurch_2996166

**Current baggage figures for commercial airlines.** Airline rules change. The Participant Guide says to check the airline before you fly. United, American, and Air New Zealand are named in ASC-Travel-0010 as honoring the USAP allowance when you travel from Christchurch.

## 2. TSA, IATA, and New Zealand rules

**TSA tools page.** Source: https://www.tsa.gov/travel/security-screening/whatcanibring/items/tools (read through a summarizer, not verified against the raw page)

- "Tools 7 inches or shorter may be allowed in carry-on baggage."
- Power tools and tools longer than 7 in must go in checked bags. The final call rests with the TSA officer.
- The page does not mention multimeters or test equipment. Meters are not banned on the page as read. Inference: a multimeter is not a banned tool in either bag, but confirm.

**TSA lithium batteries (secondary summaries; the official page returned 404 on my fetch).** Source for the summary: https://eepower.com/news/tsa-bans-lithium-batteries-from-checked-bags/ (secondary). The TSA page I tried (batteries-portable-chargers) returned 404. Treat these as secondary.

- Spare, uninstalled lithium batteries, including power banks, go in carry-on only.
- Batteries installed in a device of 100 Wh or less can travel in carry-on and checked bags. Spares cannot be checked.
- Terminals need protection (tape, a case, or a bag).
- Spare batteries of 101-160 Wh need airline approval, and there is a two-battery cap.
- Source reference: https://www.tsa.gov/travel/security-screening/whatcanibring/flammables?page=3 (search result, not read in full). OPEN for exact text.

**Installed batteries in a meter in checked bags.** Inference: a meter with an installed alkaline AA or small lithium cell is normally fine in checked luggage. I did not confirm this against a TSA or IATA source for this trip. OPEN. The cargo route makes this less important, because the ice flight is cargo-palletized.

**IATA.** I did not look up IATA DGR for this pass. OPEN.

**New Zealand customs and biosecurity (MPI).** The official MPI pages I tried returned empty or 404. Secondary sources (search snippets only) say:

- Every traveller must complete the NZ Traveller Declaration. Source: https://nzpocketguide.com/arrival-advice-biosecurity-customs-new-zealand/ (secondary).
- Used equipment and outdoor gear that has touched soil or water are listed as risk items. Declare them. Source: https://mpi.govt.nz/dmsdocument/2974/direct (older MPI document, 2015 or 2020 era).
- Undeclared risk items: fine of at least NZ$400 (secondary; check current MPI figure). OPEN.
- Participant Guide Ch. 3 says MPI "is now assessing a fee (average NZ$7) to clean and fumigate items left in Christchurch while you are in Antarctica." This is an official USAP source, but its date is older.
- Whether a test meter or probe set needs a special declaration beyond the standard form: OPEN. Tell MPI the gear will be cleaned and dry.

## 3. Leaving personal property on station

**No written USAP donation or abandonment policy found.**

- Search and fetch did not find any USAP text on donating personal equipment or on abandoned personal property at McMurdo. OPEN.
- The on-ice storage policy (AIL-07-1, effective 2007) is aimed at grantee research groups and program materials, not personal tools. Source: https://www.usap.gov/USAPgov/proposalInformation/documents/USAP%20On-Ice%20Storage%20Policy%20(AIL-07-1).pdf (not read in full; from search). Its 2014 revision is STPS-SOP-0016 per the search snippet. Read it before assuming it does not apply.
- Informal "skua" system: Antarctic Sun (April 2015) describes McMurdo residents collecting items that participants leave behind for future residents. Source: https://antarcticsun.usap.gov/features/4153 (the page did not resolve on my fetch, so the description is from the search snippet only). Inference: this is informal. It is not a property transfer, and I found no rule that makes it one.

**Shipping personal gear by cargo.** Participant Guide Ch. 3 and Ch. 4 say:

- Personal baggage is handled by the traveller unless it is checked as air cargo.
- Government transportation may not be used to ship personal goods for unauthorized personal business. Ch. 3 under "Commercial or other business activities." Donating to the station is not personal business, but whether the government accepts gear as cargo is OPEN.
- Retrograde (return) science cargo goes out by ship unless air is justified. Ch. 4. Personal retrograde is not described in the excerpt I read.

**Practical reading (inference only).** The only concrete written route is: ask ASC property or the McMurdo cargo office (Movement Control Center) in writing whether a utility mechanic can leave a personal meter kit for station use, and how it would be tagged. Do not assume the skua pile is an approved route.

## 4. Cold effects on test gear (weak sourcing; treat as practical guidance)

- Alkaline AA: Duracell's Procell spec lists operation down to -20 C. Source: https://cdw.com/product/duracell-procell-alkaline-aa-2100mah-1.5v-battery-144-carton/5546028 (secondary). Capacity drops sharply in cold. I did not get a capacity number at -20 C from a primary datasheet. OPEN.
- Lithium primary AA (Energizer L91, lithium iron disulfide): listed operating range -40 C to 60 C. Source: https://energizer.com/apac/wp-content/uploads/sites/18/2024/07/Energizer-OEM-Lithium-L91-Sell-Sheet-CN-EN%202024.01.pdf (search snippet; the capacity-vs-temperature chart values did not come through). Lithium primaries hold capacity in cold better than alkaline. This is the strongest cold-battery option I found.
- Lithium-ion (rechargeable meter batteries): Battery University, quoted on a forum, says rate capability at or below -20 C drops because electrolyte impedance rises. Source: https://www.candlepowerforums.com/goto/post?id=4362228 (secondary; forum quote). Do not charge Li-ion below freezing.
- LCD: I did not find a primary source on LCD response at low temperature. Inference from general display behavior: the display slows and fades in deep cold, and it can fog or condense when you bring it indoors. OPEN for measured data. Practical advice: keep the meter inside a jacket or pocket between readings, and let it warm up before you read it.
- Probe batteries (Bluetooth HVAC probes): not searched. OPEN. Manufacturer temperature specs are the place to check.
- Do Not Freeze rule: the Participant Guide's DNF rule means electronics that cannot tolerate freezing should not go in the checked bags on the ice flight. Whether a meter counts is OPEN. Ask the ASC cargo office.
